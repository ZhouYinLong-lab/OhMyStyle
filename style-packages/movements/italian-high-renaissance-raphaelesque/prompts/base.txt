Paint an original [SUBJECT] as a sixteenth-century central Italian painting.
Start with careful drawing: proportioned bodies, clear joints, quiet gestures,
and drapery that follows weight. Let the subject determine the arrangement: it
may be a portrait, a devotional scene, a myth, a landscape, a domestic moment,
or a small narrative group. Build depth with overlapping forms and receding
planes; use architecture only if the subject needs it, and do not force a
symmetrical hall, a central pair, or a philosopher assembly. Keep the light
open and the colors mineral: warm earth, muted blue, red, green, pale stone,
and controlled shadow. The result should feel like a painted surface with a
clear human action, not a glossy digital render or a reconstruction of a
famous fresco.
