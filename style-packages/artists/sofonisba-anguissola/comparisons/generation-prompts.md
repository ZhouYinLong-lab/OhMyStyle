# Generation prompts and visual review

## Shared subject

Three adult colleagues in contemporary plain clothing quietly review one folded map around a small table. One points to a route, one follows the gesture, and one listens. Half-length group portrait; horizontal 16:9.

## Base package image

Use the package's usual portrait treatment: individualized faces, readable hands and gestures, soft even illumination, warm natural tones, thin blended paint, restrained cloth detail, and a quiet room. This sample does not load the optional art direction.

## Direction image

Edit the base image while preserving the same three colleagues, faces, contemporary clothing, one folded map, room, and painterly treatment. Strengthen the shared-action focus: make the map and pointing hand a foreground anchor; arrange the three faces in a close, shallow arc above it so their different expressions and attention connect naturally to the marked route. Keep the pointing finger on the map and the other two people's attention directed to that point, with one subtle responsive expression. Reduce background detail and table clutter. Add no object, person, costume, setting detail, story, text, or logo.

## Review

- **Subject preservation:** both images show the same three adult colleagues and the same map activity, clothing, and room.
- **Visible rule:** the direction edit brings the faces and hand closer to the map in a tighter group crop and reduces the visual presence of the background.
- **Remaining limitation:** both images already have similar gaze and hand relationships; the edit creates a modest rather than categorical difference. The direction needs probes with different sitter counts, activities, and requested framings before its value is clear.
- **Method:** image-to-image edit keeps subject and framing close, but does not establish repeatability across models or independent generations. Formal visual review stays pending.

## Source boundary

The [National Museum in Poznań collection record for *The Chess Game*](https://mnp.art.pl/sofonisba-anguissola-gra-w-szachy-1555) documents a multi-person scene organized around a shared activity. This direction extracts only the relation between attention, gesture, and shared focus. It does not use the source image as a generation input or copy its chess scene, figures, sitter count, clothing, or setting.
