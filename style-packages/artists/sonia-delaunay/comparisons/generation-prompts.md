# Generation prompts and visual review

## Shared subject

Exactly one hip-length cobalt wool jacket displayed front-on on a plain dress form, with lapels, long sleeves, one central closure, two square patch pockets, and visible panel seams. Coordinated red, yellow, cobalt, and cream design; horizontal 16:9.

## Base package image

Use Sonia Delaunay's broad package treatment: simultaneous warm-cool color contrast, overlapping circles, arcs, and bands, crisp tactile color shapes, and a few neutral intervals. This sample does not load the optional art direction.

## Direction image

Edit the base image while preserving the same jacket, dress form, frontal pose, framing, proportions, plain warm-white background, lapels, center button, two patch pockets, sleeves, and palette. Change only the color pattern. Place geometric arcs and blocks inside the actual jacket panels. Keep lapels distinct and pocket patterns inside pocket boundaries, with pocket stitching visible. Align a few related edges across actual shoulder and body seams without crossing openings or erasing seams. Give sleeves their own longitudinal rhythm. Keep color shapes tailored to the cut, with restrained cream intervals and a tactile painted wool surface. Add no objects, text, or logos.

## Review

- **Subject preservation:** both images show one jacket on one dress form with matching framing, garment features, and palette.
- **Visible rule:** the base image uses broad arcs that flow across the body and pockets. In the direction image, arcs are segmented by body panels, sleeves have longitudinal blocks, lapels stay separate, and each pocket contains its own color pattern.
- **Remaining limitation:** the image edit subtly changes seam and pocket geometry. It is a useful directional illustration, not a controlled pixel-level comparison. The directional pattern still has dense color coverage and could use more quiet intervals in another iteration.
- **Method:** image-to-image edit keeps the subject and framing close, but does not establish repeatability across models, jacket cuts, materials, or independent generations. Formal visual review stays pending.

## Source boundary

The [MoMA artist record for Sonia Delaunay](https://www.moma.org/artists/1480-sonia-delaunay) documents her work across painting, textiles, clothing, and accessories, and discusses simultaneous contrast. The [National Gallery of Art record for *Simultaneous Patterns for Cloth*](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/199084-simultaneous-patterns-cloth) grounds the textile context. This direction applies color rhythm to user-specified garment construction; it does not copy a specific garment, textile, or composition.
