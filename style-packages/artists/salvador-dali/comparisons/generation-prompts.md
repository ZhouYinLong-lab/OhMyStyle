# Generation prompts and visual review

## Shared subject

One small red enamel kettle with an arched handle and short spout on an ivory plinth. A single lamp at upper left casts a long shadow to the right on a warm pale plaster wall. Horizontal 16:9, no other objects.

## Base package image

Render the kettle intact, near-realistically, with one ordinary kettle-shaped shadow and a lucid sparse composition. Do not create an optical double image.

## Direction image

Edit the base image while keeping the physical kettle, plinth, wall, floor, lighting, and frame unchanged. Reshape only the far edge of the attached shadow to subtly suggest the profile of a perched raven facing right. Keep the kettle's handle, body, and spout identifiable within the near part of the shadow; add no bird anatomy or physical second object.

## Review

- **Object remains singular and intact:** both images show one red kettle with the same handle, body, and spout; no bird is present as a separate object.
- **Visible direction rule:** the base shadow is an ordinary projection of the kettle. In the direction image the far contour reads as a raven-like head and beak joined to the kettle-shaped shadow mass.
- **Light consistency:** the shadow remains attached to the same object and extends rightward from the upper-left light source.
- **Method limitation:** the direction image is an edit of the baseline. This single sample cannot establish that the shadow will preserve contact geometry or make the requested second reading across other objects and models; review remains pending.

## Source boundary

The [Dalí Museum's *Masterworks: A Closer Look*](https://thedali.org/dalis-masterwork-a-closer-look/) explains how, in *The Hallucinogenic Toreador*, shadows of repeated forms contribute to a second image. This direction abstracts the double-reading mechanism into one user-requested object's shadow only; no source artwork image, Venus figure, bullfighter, or original composition is used.
